Reading the elements

Wood 38% — Peers. At 38%, Wood is the chart's largest single force, carried by the day stem, day branch, hour branch, and hidden stems within those branches. For a Yi Wood man this means he is never short of companions, parallels, or competitors — the world reflects his own nature back at him frequently. The strength here is mutual reinforcement; the risk is that too many peer-type forces in a chart already under pressure from Metal and Earth can dilute rather than consolidate.

Earth 25% — Wealth. Earth arrives at 25%, carried in the year stem, hour stem, and the hidden layers of the month branch — making Wealth (both the Steward and Entrepreneur archetypes) a persistent, ground-level presence. He is a man who is rarely far from money questions, business negotiations, or practical resource management. The challenge is that a weak Yi Wood struggling to hold its shape under Metal's pressure does not always have the root strength to carry this much wealth without outside support.

Metal 25% — Officer. Metal also claims 25%, concentrated in the month pillar's stem and branch — the most powerful structural position in the chart. This is the Governor archetype in double form: institutional authority, external standards, rules, and the ongoing expectation that he perform and conform to external criteria. Metal cuts Wood; its weight here is real, and it is the primary reason the chart classifies as a Weak Self despite Wood's surface abundance.

Fire 12% — Output. Fire sits at a modest 12%, alive only in the year branch and its hidden stems. Fire represents his creative and expressive output — the Creator and Maverick archetypes. Its thinness means self-expression and creative boldness are available but not automatically sustained; they require favorable seasonal or decade-level conditions to ignite into full productivity.

Water 0% — Resource. Water is 0% — not a single drop in any stem or branch of the four pillars. For a Yi Wood chart Water is the Resource element: the nourishing, stabilizing force that feeds the vine from below. Its complete absence is the most consequential structural fact in this chart. There is no built-in wellspring of institutional support, mentorship energy, or quiet inner renewal; whatever composure and backing he has developed came entirely through effort and external seeking, not from a natal reserve.

The Missing Root: Water at ZeroA vine without water does not simply grow more slowly — it grows anxiously, reaching in every direction for any source of moisture. The total absence of Water (Resource) in this chart means that the one element most capable of calming the Governor's Metal pressure and feeding Yi Wood's growth simply does not exist in the natal foundation. This makes the chart structurally dependent on Water appearing in the luck pillars and annual years — and makes every decade that carries it feel, in retrospect, like a decisive turning point.
